# Ancient Scripts Research and Provenance

## Research basis

The platform is designed around the fact that Ancient North Arabian is a group of scripts and text traditions rather than a single uniformly deciphered language. OCIANA documents Dadanitic, Dumaitic, Taymanitic, Hismaic, Safaitic and multiple Thamudic categories, with tens of thousands of graffiti and inscriptions.

## OCR and translation research

The implementation strategy incorporates patterns from open research projects for cuneiform OCR and ancient-language translation: document/glyph detection, transliteration normalization, low-resource neural sequence-to-sequence learning, retrieval-augmented translation, lexicon lookup, uncertainty handling and ensemble evaluation.

## Provenance rules

Every imported dataset or algorithm must record source URL, repository, license, version/date, attribution requirements and whether redistribution is permitted. Code is not copied from a project merely because it is public. License-compatible implementations may be adapted; restrictive or non-redistributable projects remain external references.

## Translation safety

Ancient-script translation is scholarly inference. The engine must preserve alternate readings, lacunae, damaged signs, uncertain phoneme values and competing translations. A high-confidence language-model output must never erase the underlying epigraphic evidence.
